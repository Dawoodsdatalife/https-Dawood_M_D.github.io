Product case study · concept and project framing

Making internal knowledge easier to find, and safer to trust.

A product-thinking walkthrough of my Enterprise RAG Assistant with Citations & Guardrails, turning a technical build into a user problem, an MVP, prioritization choices, and measurable outcomes.

Honest scope: this is a proposed product plan. It is not formal customer research, and every figure below is a pilot target, not an achieved result.

01 · Context and problem

Employees often need answers buried across many internal documents. Manual search is slow, and an AI answer without evidence is hard to verify. The assistant retrieves relevant passages and answers with citations, protected by input and output guardrails.

How might we help employees find reliable answers from approved documents, while making it easy to verify where each answer came from?

02 · Users and jobs to be done

  • Knowledge worker: needs a policy, process, or technical answer quickly, with the source.
  • Team lead or knowledge owner: wants people to use current, approved material.
  • Administrator: needs visibility into document coverage, retrieval quality, and failures.
Job to be done: when I have a work question, help me find the most relevant approved information and show the evidence, so I can act with confidence.

03 · Discovery plan

I would validate the problem before expanding scope. My experience as a co-founder, interviewing industrial clients about needs, specifications, and budgets, shapes how I would run this:

  • Interview 5–8 target employees across at least two functions about how they find information today.
  • Observe real information-seeking tasks and record time, search steps, and moments of doubt.
  • Collect common question types and identify which sources are authoritative.
  • Test a prototype with representative questions and ask users to rate usefulness and source clarity.

04 · Proposed MVP and user journey

  1. The user asks a question in plain language.
  2. The system checks the input and retrieves passages from the approved index.
  3. The assistant answers from those passages and attaches citations.
  4. Output checks screen for unsupported or unsafe content.
  5. The user opens a citation to verify, or sees a clear “not enough evidence” reply.

MVP scope: a limited approved corpus, retrieval, cited answers, basic guardrails, citation opening, and feedback capture. Defer complex permissions, multilingual support, broad integrations, and advanced analytics.

07 · Risks and mitigations

Incorrect or outdated source material

Show document title and date, and give content owners a process to update the index.

Unsupported generated claims

Require evidence checks and a clear abstention path when retrieval confidence is low.

Access-control leakage

Enforce document permissions during retrieval and validate before any real deployment.

Low adoption

Integrate into an existing workflow only after discovery shows where users need it.

Misleading metrics

Combine quantitative measures with human review and interviews.

08 · What I would do next

Validate the highest-frequency knowledge tasks, build a representative evaluation set, and baseline current search time and answer confidence. Then prioritize by observed failure patterns, such as poor retrieval, missing citations, stale documents, or unclear abstentions, rather than adding features without evidence.

Takeaway: the value is not “AI answers questions.” It is helping people finish information tasks faster while they can still verify the answer and see when the system doesn’t know.
